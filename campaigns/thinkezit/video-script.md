# Personalized Walk & Talk — Michael + Barry
Target runtime: ~2 minutes

Hey Michael. Hey Barry.

I wanted to show you something, because this is exactly what I meant when we talked about going after a market instead of just telling everybody that ThinkEzIT does IT.

Michael, think about the average law firm around Chambersburg. They are holding client files, email, financial information, case strategy and deadlines — and their attorneys make money when they can work. If technology goes down, that is not an IT problem. It is lost time, client risk and potentially a very uncomfortable conversation.

So the first message should not be, “Hey, we sell managed services.”

It should be useful.

Barry, what if the first thing a firm gets from ThinkEzIT is a two-minute way to pressure-test six things: recovery, email and identity, confidentiality, patching, visibility and continuity?

Now they can see where they stand before anybody asks them to buy something.

And Michael, this is where your 30-day trial gets interesting. Instead of asking a firm to move everything, you can say: pick the one computer the office really depends on. Let us proactively manage that system for 30 days and show you what the experience is like.

That is a very different conversation.

We are not leading with tools. We are leading with the consequence the client understands, giving them something useful, and then giving them a low-risk next step.

And the important part is that this message sounds like it was built for a law firm — because it was.

Now imagine the same capability translated for healthcare, where the language, consequences and decision triggers are completely different.

That is what I wanted you both to see.

## Visual grammar
Use real Walk & Talk footage. Blue shirt preferred; black shirt fallback. Camera perspective approximately waist high and pulled back. Vary framing/distance subtly (about 10–20%) when addressing Michael versus Barry so the viewer subconsciously reads two walking companions. Avoid close lip visibility and avoid a cinematic/staged feel.
