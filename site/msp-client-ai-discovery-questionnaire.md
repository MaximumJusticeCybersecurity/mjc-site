# MSP Client AI Discovery Questionnaire

Use this during a QBR, vCIO/vCISO review, security assessment, or AI-governance discovery conversation.  The purpose is to understand where AI is already influencing decisions and where accountability, evidence, or control may be unclear.

Do not collect confidential, regulated, privileged, classified, export-controlled, or unnecessary personal data in a discovery worksheet.

## 1. AI inventory

1. Where is AI already being used in the organization?
2. Which AI tools are officially approved?
3. Which AI tools are being used outside the approved technology stack?
4. Which business units rely on AI most heavily?
5. Which vendors have recently added AI features to existing products?
6. Which AI capabilities are embedded in security, IT, HR, finance, sales, customer service, legal, or operations systems?

## 2. Data access

1. What company, customer, employee, operational, security, financial, or regulated data can each AI system access?
2. Is data sent to external model providers?
3. Is company/customer data used to train or improve a model?
4. What retention rules apply to prompts, outputs, embeddings, logs, and retrieved content?
5. Can AI reach information that the user invoking it could not otherwise access?
6. Are tenant, role, department, and customer boundaries enforced?

## 3. Decision influence

1. Which decisions does AI recommend?
2. Which decisions does AI materially influence even if a person approves the final action?
3. Which actions can AI execute directly?
4. Which AI-assisted decisions can affect customers, systems, data, money, contracts, compliance, safety, or reputation?
5. Which decisions would be difficult to reverse if the AI were wrong?
6. Which decisions would an executive want explained after an incident?

## 4. Human authority

1. Who owns each high-consequence AI-assisted decision?
2. Does that person have the authority appropriate to the consequence?
3. What information does the reviewer receive before approval?
4. Can the reviewer realistically disagree with or modify the AI recommendation?
5. Are approvals meaningful review or primarily a click-through step?
6. When does a decision escalate to security, privacy, legal, compliance, finance, executive leadership, or another accountable authority?

## 5. Evidence

1. What evidence supports material AI recommendations?
2. Can reviewers inspect the evidence rather than only the AI's explanation?
3. Are sources current, relevant, authoritative, and independent enough for the decision?
4. How are contradictory sources handled?
5. What happens when evidence is missing?
6. Can the AI/system abstain, defer, or request more evidence?
7. How are assumptions distinguished from facts?

## 6. Risk If Wrong

For each material AI-assisted decision, ask:

- What happens if it is wrong?
- Who is harmed or financially affected?
- Is the action reversible?
- Could it create a security incident?
- Could it expose regulated or sensitive data?
- Could it violate a contract, policy, law, or regulatory obligation?
- Could it materially interrupt operations?
- Could it affect safety, employment, access, credit, care, or another high-impact human outcome?

## 7. Decision record

1. Could the organization reconstruct why an AI-assisted decision was made six months later?
2. Is the original recommendation preserved?
3. Are material inputs and evidence preserved?
4. Is the human reviewer and final human determination preserved separately?
5. Is the resulting action tied to the decision record?
6. Are model/vendor/version and timestamps available where material?
7. Are retention periods appropriate for the consequence and obligations involved?

## 8. AI failure and incident readiness

1. What happens when the AI is unavailable?
2. What happens when it produces conflicting or obviously incorrect output?
3. What happens when it recommends a destructive or high-impact action?
4. Can AI-triggered actions be reversed?
5. Who investigates an AI-related incident?
6. What evidence would be preserved before logs rotate or systems change?
7. Does the organization have an escalation path to the AI vendor/model provider?

## 9. Business value

1. What specific business outcome is each AI investment expected to improve?
2. What baseline existed before AI was introduced?
3. Which KPIs demonstrate that the AI is creating value?
4. Are token, model, integration, licensing, support, and oversight costs included in the business case?
5. Has AI reduced labor or merely shifted work into review, correction, integration, or exception handling?
6. Which AI tools or processes are duplicative?
7. Which AI investments would the organization stop if value cannot be demonstrated?

## 10. MSP service opportunity

Ask the client:

1. Would you value help inventorying and governing AI already in use?
2. Would you value independent AI vendor due diligence before purchase or renewal?
3. Would you value a decision-review process for high-consequence AI recommendations?
4. Would you value evidence-retention standards for AI-assisted decisions?
5. Would you prefer a one-time assessment, recurring managed service, QBR-integrated review, or advisory engagement?
6. Which executive or budget owner would sponsor this work?
