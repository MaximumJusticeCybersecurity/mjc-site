Your service desk, security stack, vendors, technicians, and clients are already using AI. The hard question is not whether AI can generate an answer. It is whether you have enough evidence, authority, and decision history to defend the action you take because of that answer.

  1. Should it? Should AI be influencing this decision at all?

  3. Can we defend it later? Could we reconstruct who decided, on what evidence, and why?
10-minute AI decision review scan

How much human review does this decision deserve?

This is a bounded advisory screen, not a universal trust score. It looks at consequence, evidence, authority, reversibility, and recordkeeping. No assessment answers are sent to MJC.

1. What authority does the AI have?
2. What happens if the decision is wrong?
3. How strong is the evidence behind the recommendation?
4. What about contradictory or missing evidence?
5. Who owns the final decision?
6. How reversible is the action?
7. What human approval exists before action?
8. Could you reconstruct the decision later?

    Important: this screen identifies review pressure; it does not determine that an AI recommendation is correct, compliant, safe, or legally defensible.

    Five-minute check

    Before acting on an important AI recommendation

    Use these six questions in a service desk, SOC, change review, QBR, vendor review, or executive discussion.

    1

    Should it?

    Should AI be making or materially influencing this decision at all?

    2

    Evidence?

    What evidence supports the recommendation, and can a responsible reviewer inspect it?

    3

    Contradiction?

    What evidence is missing, conflicting, stale, dependent, or simply assumed?

    4

    Risk if wrong?

    What happens to the client, MSP, system, data, finances, obligations, or people if the answer fails?

    5

    Authority?

    Who has enough evidence, accountability, and authority to approve the action?

    6

    Record?

    Could you reconstruct the recommendation, evidence, human judgment, and resulting action later?

    Human at the helm

    Increase human authority as Risk If Wrong rises.

    Human oversight should increase with Risk If Wrong. Human oversight is not a universal rubber stamp. The level of review should reflect consequence, reversibility, evidence sufficiency, and the authority required for the decision.

    Lower consequence

    Bounded automation may be appropriate when actions are low-impact, observable, reversible, and well understood.

    Keep logging and clear boundaries.
    Moderate consequence

    Allow bounded action with monitoring, exception review, and tested rollback.

    Define escalation conditions in advance.
    High consequence

    Require qualified human authorization before the material action occurs.

    Give the reviewer evidence and a real ability to disagree.
    Critical consequence

    Require independent corroboration, explicit named authority, and stronger evidence before action.

    Consider defer, request evidence, or no action when support is insufficient.

    Do not start with the framework

    Start with the last decision that mattered.

    Whether you are talking to your own operations team or a client, these questions expose the real workflow faster than a generic AI-readiness conversation.

    Ask the operator

    • What was the last AI-assisted recommendation that could materially affect a system, customer, security posture, or business outcome?
    • What happened from recommendation to action?
    • Who owned the final decision?
    • What evidence did that person actually see?
    • What would have happened if the recommendation was wrong?

    Ask the business

    • Is this problem primarily internal to the MSP, client-facing, or both?
    • What record would an insurer, regulator, customer, board, or attorney ask for after a failure?
    • Would this be more useful as a one-time review, a managed service, part of a QBR, or platform capability?
    • What would you need to see before you would trust the process enough to use it?
    • Which existing process would it have to fit into rather than replace?
    Put it against a real decision

    Bring me one AI-assisted decision where being wrong actually matters.

    If the decision is appropriate for a bounded review, we can examine what the AI recommended, what evidence exists, what is missing, the Risk If Wrong, and what a responsible human decision record should preserve. Do not send confidential, regulated, privileged, classified, or sensitive client data in an initial email.

    Useful first message:“Max, I want to pressure-test an AI-assisted decision. The decision is [high-level description]. AI currently [recommends / executes]. The consequence if wrong is [high-level impact]. I want to understand whether this is more useful for our MSP internally, for our clients, or both.”
