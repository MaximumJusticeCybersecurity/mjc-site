Six questions worth answering before technology disrupts the day

Count one gap for each statement you cannot confidently answer “yes” to today.

Essential business and care-support operations have a known continuity path when a workstation, internet connection, network component or cloud service fails.
Accounts are protected appropriately, privileged access is limited and access is removed promptly when roles change.
Backups are monitored and recovery expectations are understood before an outage forces the test.
Workstations and common applications are inventoried, patched, monitored and protected consistently.
Phones, connectivity, email and other essential communications have continuity expectations.
The practice understands the technology vendors that touch sensitive information or critical workflows and the operational dependencies they create.
Interpretation
0–1 gaps: validate the controls and keep testing.
2–3 gaps: prioritize the dependencies that disrupt the most patient/staff activity.
4+ gaps: build a focused continuity plan before an outage chooses the priorities for you.

Take this back to the team: Which technology dependency would disrupt the most patient or staff activity if it failed tomorrow—and what is the tested recovery path?
